# Flynn's Listening Studio

A classroom app for I.S., a 3rd-grade student who is Deaf/Hard of Hearing and uses spoken language. It practices and tracks her four IEP goals for listening comprehension, auditory memory, Assistive Listening Device (ALD) use and self-advocacy.

Open `index.html` in any browser (no install). `app.html` is the same page without the document wrapper, used for the hosted version.

## Launch it in Chrome (needed for follow-along)
Follow-along word highlighting works only when the hosted link is open in Google Chrome (or Edge, or the Claude desktop app). A Chrome shortcut opens the app in Chrome every time, whatever your default browser is.

Hosted link: https://claude.ai/artifact/UQjJqoJ2Lgeh5DR3UkfgjZ

1. Open Chrome and sign in to claude.ai with the account that owns the app.
2. Go to the hosted link above.
3. Click Chrome's menu (⋮, top right), then **Cast, save, and share → Create shortcut…** (in some versions: **Save and share → Create shortcut…** or **More tools → Create shortcut…**).
4. Name it "Flynn's Listening Studio". Leave **Open as window** unchecked so it opens as a normal Chrome tab, then click **Create**.
5. The shortcut appears on the desktop (Windows/Mac) or in the launcher (Chromebook). Pin it to the taskbar or shelf if you like.

Other options: set Chrome as the device's default browser, or bookmark the link in Chrome. If the app is opened somewhere follow-along can't run, teacher mode shows a **Copy link** button to paste into Chrome. Chrome on iPad and iPhone is not supported, because Apple requires a different browser engine there.

## Using it on a classroom smartboard
- Open the hosted link in the board's browser (Chrome on the board's PC is best) and sign in to claude.ai.
- In teacher mode, set **This screen → Smartboard**. Text, buttons, answer choices, step cards and Speak-Up cards get bigger, so she can read them from across the room and tap them easily. The reading page widens to fill the board. Long-press text selection is turned off, and the screen is kept awake during lessons where the browser allows it.
- The setting is saved per device, so the board can use Smartboard display while the iPad stays regular. Very large touch screens start in Smartboard display automatically.
- Press **Full screen** (top left) to hide the browser bars. Press it again, or press Esc, to exit. If the board's browser blocks it, use the browser's own full-screen key (F11 on Windows).
- For word highlighting on the board, **Her finger** and **Pacer** work on any board. **Her voice** needs Chrome or Edge on the board's PC and a microphone close to her. A board's built-in microphone is usually too far away to hear her clearly.
- She can reach the **Next** and answer buttons, which sit low on the screen. The tabs at the top may be out of her reach, so switch activities for her.
- On a shared classroom computer, sign out of claude.ai at the end of the day so student data stays private.

## Two modes
- **Student mode** opens first, every time. She sees only her comic panels, the activity tabs and her stickers. It opens on the most recent lesson you made, or on an example lesson (a two-paragraph *Flynn's Day at the Dog Park* that you take turns reading) before you've made one.
- **Teacher mode:** press and hold **Teacher: hold** (top right) for about a second. A quick tap does nothing, so she won't wander in by accident. Here you choose the goal, passage category, theme, length, key details, how she answers and the accommodations, score trials and see **Teacher data**. The pink panels show a live **student preview**. Press **Present in student mode** or **Start student mode** to hand the screen back.
- **Teacher tools while she listens:** in student mode, press and hold **Teacher tools: hold** (bottom right) for the blurred script, the repetition counter and computer voice. You can read aloud without leaving her screen. **Score in teacher mode** takes you straight to the detail checklist.
- When she taps her answers, the trial saves on its own when she finishes. You can add support and notes later in teacher mode and press **Update saved trial**.
- The ALD **Show me** checklist and all Goal 4 classroom logging are teacher-mode only.

## Built around her interests
- **Look:** pastel blue and pink, drawn as a graphic novel. Every student screen is a comic panel with speech bubbles.
- **Flynn, her dog,** is the guide on every screen. Stickers are paw prints and music notes.
- **Themes for listening passages:** Flynn & pets, Music & singing, Comic club (graphic novels, library), Animal world.
- **Music as a memory tool:** "The Listening Song" puts the ALD steps to a beat with karaoke-style highlighting, and the "Sing it back" accommodation prompts her to rehearse details to a tune she knows.
- All stories and lyrics are original. They don't reuse characters or songs from her favorite books and artists.

## Listen & Remember: Goals 1 and 2

### My topic (leveled text)
The first option under **What I present orally**. Type any topic, such as "how bees make honey" or "the water cycle", and press **Write leveled text** (or Enter).
- **Grade level:** K through 8. After a text is written, tap any grade to see the same topic rewritten at that level. Grades with a green dot are ready and appear instantly. The grades just above and below the one you picked are written in the background, so they're usually ready by the time you tap. Other grades take up to a minute.
- **Length:** the **Paragraphs** (1–5) and **Sentences in each paragraph** (5–9) sliders work here too. Changing them rewrites the text at the current grade.
- **Measured level:** each text shows its Flesch-Kincaid grade, so you can see whether the leveling landed where you wanted.
- **Questions:** recall questions, the main idea, and the highlighted key details in your script are made from the text automatically. Everything else works with these texts as with the stories: taking turns, she reads it herself, word highlighting, scoring and data.

**Where the facts come from.** The page can't search the web (claude.ai doesn't let pages load other websites), so there are two ways to use it:
- **Topic only:** Claude writes from its own general knowledge. It is told to use only well-established facts found in reputable children's references and to leave out anything uncertain. Nothing is looked up live, so the app labels the text **Check the facts before teaching** and names references to check against.
- **Use only facts from a trusted source:** paste text from a source you trust, such as Britannica Kids, National Geographic Kids, a museum or science-agency page, or your textbook. The passage uses only facts from that text and is labeled that way. If the source is too short for the length you chose, the text comes back shorter and says why.

**School-appropriate topics:** topics that aren't right for a classroom (violence, weapons, drugs, adult content and similar) are declined with a short message. Always read a text before using it with a student.

**Using Claude:** writing a text uses your own Claude account and usage. The first time, claude.ai asks you to allow it. It works only on the hosted claude.ai link, not in `index.html` opened as a file. Each lesson remembers its topic, pasted source and the grades already written, so toggling still works after a reload.
1. Choose the goal, what you present (3–5 sentences, a **passage**, classroom directions, teacher information), the theme and how many key details to check (4, 5, 6 or all). Goal 2 hides the 3–5 sentence option because it targets extended information.
2. **Passage length:** two sliders set the number of paragraphs (1–5) and the sentences in each paragraph (5–9). Each theme has its own multi-paragraph story: *Flynn's Day at the Dog Park*, *The Big Concert*, *The Comic Club Contest* and *A Visit to the Animal Rescue*.
3. **How you present it:**
   - **We take turns reading (she sees the text):** the passage appears as a graphic-novel page with one comic panel per paragraph. Choose turns by paragraph or by sentence, and who reads first. A bar at the bottom says whose turn it is (pink for her, blue for you), and she taps **Next turn** after each one. Turn on **Blur my turns** to keep your parts blurred, so she reads her parts and listens to yours. That keeps listening practice in the shared reading.
   - **She reads it herself:** the same graphic-novel page, but every part is hers. She reads the pink paragraph or sentence aloud and taps **Next part**. Choose whether one paragraph or one sentence is highlighted at a time. You can follow along with the blurred script in Teacher tools.
   - **Word highlighting (follow along):** for *We take turns* and *She reads it herself*, pick a highlighting mode. Words already read get a pink underline, and the current word is gold. Tap any word to move the highlight there.
     - **Her finger:** she slides her finger under the words (or taps one) and each word lights up. Works everywhere, including iPad, with no microphone.
     - **Pacer:** tap **Start pacer** and the highlight moves word by word at the speed you set (20–120 words a minute). It moves on to the next part by itself when she is reading alone. Works everywhere, including iPad.
     - **Her voice (microphone):** the app listens and highlights each word as she says it. On the claude.ai link this works only in Chrome, Edge or the Claude desktop app, not on iPad. The page receives only text, never a recording. Speech recognition can miss words with young readers, so treat it as a support, not a score. Check your district's policy on student voice data.

#### Voice highlighting on an iPad
Apple doesn't allow the claude.ai page's microphone feature in iPad browsers. The workaround is to host `index.html` on a regular web address, such as a school website, Google Sites embed, or GitHub Pages, and open it in Safari. Outside claude.ai, the app uses the iPad's built-in speech recognition instead.
- On the iPad, turn on **Settings → General → Keyboard → Enable Dictation** (and Siri if asked). Allow the microphone when Safari asks.
- Apple's speech recognition may send audio to Apple to process it, so check your district's policy first.
- When opened this way, data is saved only on that iPad in Safari, not to your claude.ai account. Safari can clear a website's saved data if it isn't opened for several weeks. Use **Teacher data → Backup** regularly, or add the page to the Home Screen.
- I tested this path only in an emulator, not on a real iPad. Try a short passage first.

   - **I read it aloud (text hidden):** the original listening task. Flynn shows "Listening ears on!" while you read.
4. **The teacher script** (teacher mode, or **Teacher tools** in student mode) is blurred until you press and hold. Key details are highlighted, each turn is labeled *Me* or *Her*, and `/ pause /` marks show where to stop when chunking is on. In turn-taking mode, the computer voice reads only the current turn.
5. Count repetitions with **+ Repeated**. The goal allows one, so a second repetition marks the trial as not meeting criterion.
6. She recalls in one of two ways:
   - **She tells me:** tap each key detail she recalls, and mark the main idea for Goal 2.
   - **She taps answer choices:** one question per detail (plus the main idea for Goal 2), with visual right/try-again feedback.
7. Pick the support given, add notes and **Save trial**.

Each saved trial records how the passage was presented (for example, "took turns by paragraph, teacher started"), so data from shared reading can be told apart from listening-only trials.

**Meets criterion:** 4 or more key details, 80% or higher, no more than 1 repetition, plus the correct main idea for Goal 2.

**Comic-panel graphic organizer:** when this accommodation is on, a comic panel sits to the right of each paragraph on the reading page (below it on narrow screens). Each panel has a picture of that paragraph, its big idea in a few words, and 2–3 key words in speech bubbles. Claude draws the pictures when you turn the accommodation on, write a new text or switch to a new passage. Use **Draw comic panels** / **Redraw comic panels** in teacher mode to make them again. Until a drawing is ready, or where Claude isn't available, each panel shows a simple picture with words taken from the paragraph. Drawings are saved with the lesson, and with each grade of a leveled text, so they don't need redrawing. Each drawing is checked before it's shown: only plain shapes are kept, and anything that could run code or load outside content is removed. Look over the panels before showing them to her. During blurred teacher turns, the panel for your paragraph is blurred too. The recall boxes after listening still appear as before.

**Choosing a picture (picture picker):** in teacher mode, tap **Choose picture** on any comic panel (or next to the panel's row under *Pictures for the panels*). A side panel opens for that paragraph, laid out like Canva's photo search:
- The search box is pre-filled with a picture idea for the paragraph, and suggestion chips under it come from the paragraph's key words. Tap a chip to search for it.
- **Tabs:** **Canva AI** (Canva makes 2 new pictures from your words per search; **More results** makes 2 more), **Drive folder** (searches the pictures in your approved Google Drive folder by name, with thumbnails), and **Upload** (a picture from the device or the iPad camera).
- **Filters** (☰ next to the search box): Style (Cartoon, Watercolor, Photo-like), Colour (pastel, bright, natural, any) and Orientation (horizontal or square).
- Grey placeholders show while pictures are found. Each result is saved to the app's storage as soon as it arrives, so it's ready on every device, and **one tap puts it in the panel**. A check mark shows the panel's current picture, and pictures you've used stay at the top of the Canva AI tab. **Next panel ›** moves straight to the next paragraph; Esc or ✕ closes the picker.
- The picker is teacher-only. Canva AI pictures take about 20 seconds each and use your Canva AI allowance.

**Automatic Canva pictures:** when Canva is connected to your claude.ai account, turning on the comic-panel organizer (or making a new passage or leveled text while it's on) does the whole workflow on its own. Claude writes a caption, key words and a picture description for each paragraph. Then the app asks Canva to generate a 4:3 picture for each paragraph, waits for each one (about 20 seconds per picture), saves it privately with the app, and puts it in that paragraph's panel.
- **More options** asks Canva for another picture for that panel. Tap any option's thumbnail to use it (up to 4 per panel). **Stop** ends the run.
- Canva sends the app a small preview (about 200 × 150 pixels), which looks fine in a panel on the iPad. For a sharp picture on the SMART Board, use **Open in Canva**, download the full-size picture (about 1456 × 1088) and add it with **Upload**.
- Each generated picture is also saved in your Canva account and uses your Canva AI allowance. Panels that already have a picture are skipped. The first time, claude.ai asks you to allow the page to use Canva. Look the pictures over before showing them to her.

**Real pictures for the comic panels (Canva, uploads, Google Drive):** in teacher mode, with the comic-panel organizer on, open **Pictures for the panels**:
1. **Copy Canva request** copies a ready-made request listing the current passage panel by panel. Paste it into a new Claude chat with the Canva connector connected. Claude makes a Canva design with one 4:3 page per panel. In Canva, choose **Share → Download → PNG → All pages**. On an iPad, the download is a zip file; open it in the Files app to unzip.
2. Add the pictures to the app:
   - **Upload pictures:** choose several images at once and they go into Panel 1, 2, 3… in filename order. Each panel also has its own **Upload** button and a **Remove** button.
   - **Google Drive folder:** paste a folder link (or type its name) and press **Show pictures in folder**. Then use **Match by name** (pictures are matched to paragraphs by the words in their file names), **Use in order** (the first picture goes to Panel 1, and so on), or pick a picture for each panel from its list. The first time, claude.ai asks you to allow the page to use Google Drive.
- Pictures are resized and saved privately with the app, so they show on every device you use (iPad, SMART Board), in student mode and after a reload. When the file is opened directly, they are kept in that browser instead. A picture replaces the drawn picture for its panel; the caption and key words stay. **Redraw comic panels** keeps your pictures. Switching a leveled text to another grade keeps the same pictures when the paragraph count matches.

**Accommodations you can switch on (saved with each trial):** ALD on and checked, preferential seating, "Listen for…" preview chips (Who? Where? How many?), chunking with pauses, slower rate, visual comic recap after recall, look-back (the passage stays available during questions), sing-it-back strategy, comic-panel graphic organizer, check for understanding, reduced background noise.

New passages are built from hand-written story frames, direction sets and fact lists, with names, days, colors, numbers, songs, books and so on filled in at random. Each tap of **New passage** gives a new version, and every answer choice is generated to match.

## My Listening Device: Goal 3
1. **Learn it:** comic panels explaining what the ALD is, why it helps, when to use it and what to do if it's not working, followed by step-by-step panels.
2. **Sing it:** The Listening Song.
3. **Put steps in order:** she taps shuffled step cards into numbered slots (4, 6 or all steps). Scored by position.
4. **When do I use it?:** classroom scenes. She chooses "Yes, use it!" or "Not needed now" and sees why.
5. **Why does it help?:** questions about purpose and benefit.
6. **Show me:** while she uses her real device, the teacher marks each step *on her own / prompted / not yet*. Only steps she does on her own count.

Edit the step list in **Teacher data → Settings** to match her real device, for example "Roger" or "my FM". The lesson, song cards, sequencing and checklist all update. Accommodations: picture icons, song reminder, modeled first, fewer choices, read aloud, hands-on with her real device.

## Speak Up!: Goal 4
- **My Speak-Up cards:** big cards she can tap to show full screen ("Please turn on the mic." "My battery is low." "Can you say that again, please?" …).
- **Practice scenarios:** "Is there a listening problem?" (recognizing the need), then "What could you say or do?" (choosing a kind, clear request over a passive or rude choice). Scenarios include ones where nothing is wrong, so she also learns when she *doesn't* need to speak up.
- **Real classroom log (the goal data):** for each real opportunity, record the setting, whether she recognized the need and whether she communicated it (on her own / with a prompt / not yet), and what she said. A **data period** meets the goal at 80% (4 of 5) with at least 5 opportunities. Start a new period whenever your data-collection window ends, for example weekly. A setting lets "with a prompt" count as success if your team agrees.

## Teacher data
- One card per goal: status (Building, *n* of 3 in a row, Criterion met), 3-in-a-row dots, a bar chart of recent scores against the 80% line, and the goal wording.
- Every trial and opportunity is listed with date, score, repetitions, support, accommodations, details and notes. Uncheck "Count as a data collection opportunity" to save a practice trial that doesn't affect mastery.
- **Copy data as CSV** to paste into Google Sheets or Excel. **Backup or move data** copies all records as text and restores them on another device.
- **Where data is saved:** on the hosted page, it's saved privately to the teacher's claude.ai account. When `index.html` is opened as a file, it stays in that browser. Use initials only.
